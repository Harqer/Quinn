# MIT Essay & Science Writing Framework (ES.240 / 21W.735)

## 1. The Nut Graph
- **The Hook:** Start with an engaging anecdote, surprising statistic, or counter-intuitive observation.
- **The Nut Graph:** By the 3rd or 4th paragraph, explicitly state *why* the listener should care about this topic right now. What is the core argument?

## 2. Translating Complexity
- **Analogies:** Use concrete, everyday analogies to explain complex scientific, historical, or abstract concepts.
- **The "So What?":** Constantly connect abstract ideas back to human impact. How does this science/history affect the daily life of the listener?

## 3. Audio-Specific Rules
- **Signposting:** Because the listener cannot skim or re-read, you must explicitly preview where the argument is going ("There are three reasons for this...") and summarize complex points after making them.
- **Conversational Authority:** The tone should be authoritative but accessible—like an engaging professor holding office hours, not a textbook.
